### Title:
          KFS$^4$LAM: Adaptive KeyFrame Selection with Structure-Aware Mapping for Endoscopic Gaussian Splatting SLAM
 - **Authors:** Xinwei Ju, Zhehua Mao, Tianyi Song, Danail Stoyanov, Sophia Bano, Francisco Vasconcelos
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (cs.CV)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 Gaussian-splatting Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) augments conventional SLAM with a dense, renderable 3D scene representation, enabling high-quality reconstruction and novel-view synthesis. However, its localization, reconstruction, and rendering performance depends strongly on which observations are retained for mapping. Existing systems commonly rely on fixed temporal intervals or simple pose- and overlap-based criteria for keyframe selection. In colonoscopy, non-uniform camera motion, repetitive anatomy, specularities, and visual artifacts can make these criteria unreliable, leading to either redundant keyframes or missed informative viewpoints. We present KFS$^4$LAM (KeyFrame Selection with Structure-aware mapping for endoscopic Gaussian Splatting SLAM), which couples motion-guided adaptive keyframe selection with structurally supervised mapping and restricted temporal deformation. Accumulated motion guides adaptive keyframe selection, with complementary cues suppressing redundant or unreliable candidates. Density-aware mapping with anatomical contour supervision (colon folds) emphasizes structurally informative regions, while restricted temporal deformation accommodates local tissue dynamics without unconstrained geometric drift. We evaluate KFS$^4$LAM against Gaussian-SLAM baselines on C3VD and C3VD-v2, together with cross-backbone and component ablations. KFS$^4$LAM achieves strong overall performance across trajectory, rendering, and depth metrics, with pronounced gains on more challenging C3VD-v2 dataset. In the matched comparison with SplaTAM on C3VD-v2, it reduces rotational relative pose error by 20.3% and depth RMSE by 38.4%, while improving PSNR by 0.78 dB. Cross-backbone experiments further show that the framework is not tied to a single underlying SLAM architecture. Code and visualization results are available at this https URL.
### Title:
          RTISSLAM-3D: Full 6-DoF SLAM With an In-Air 3D Imaging Sonar
 - **Authors:** Jan Steckel, Vincent Katter, Wouter Jansen
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Robotics (cs.RO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 Echolocating bats navigate dense forests using sonar alone, and in-air 3D imaging sonars now capture the entire frontal hemisphere with every pulse. Sonar SLAM, however, has so far been planar: current systems estimate a 2D trajectory and discard the elevation information in the sonar images, an assumption that does not hold on rough and sloped terrain such as a forest floor. In this paper, we introduce RTISSLAM-3D, a novel 6-DoF SLAM system for a wheeled robot that carries an in-air 3D imaging sonar, an IMU and wheel encoders. A CFAR detector on the sphere reduces every sonar image to a sparse set of 3D detections, which we use for a 3D acoustic odometry that models the shape of the sonar beam, for place recognition with polar submap descriptors, and for the verification of loop closures by 3D registration, all combined in a 6-DoF pose graph with attitude factors. We evaluated RTISSLAM-3D on a 545m drive through a forest, with a 3D lidar SLAM as reference. The loop closures reduced the horizontal error of the acoustic odometry from 1.74m to 0.28m, with a height error of 0.12m, and 94.6% of the resulting 3D sonar map lies within 0.3 m of the lidar map.
### Title:
          FLoTS-SLAM: Optical Flow-Derived Uncertainty for Monocular Dynamic SLAM with Two-Stage Optimization
 - **Authors:** Haorui Du, Lipu Zhou
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Robotics (cs.RO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 Monocular simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) remains challenging in dynamic environments. Real-time dynamic object identification in such environments is a critical problem. Recent approaches estimate per-pixel uncertainty from rendering residuals or multiview feature inconsistency to identify dynamic regions. These methods lack direct motion evidence, and their accuracy may degrade when reconstruction is inaccurate or features are ambiguous. In addition, such uncertainty merely downweights pixels in bundle adjustment (BA), so inaccurate uncertainty estimates can still impair camera pose estimation and 3D reconstruction. Accordingly, we present a monocular RGB SLAM system for dynamic scenes. Specifically, a motion uncertainty estimation method is proposed to derive uncertainty from the residual between optical flow and camera motion induced flow, directly reflecting object motion. The estimation varies with frame alignment accuracy, reducing false detections and yielding accurate pixel-level masks of dynamic regions. To make BA more robust, we estimate camera poses from high confidence pixels. These poses are then used to reproject dynamic pixels during joint refinement of poses and depth, reducing the influence of object motion on both pose and depth updates. Moreover, we provide a quantitative evaluation of dynamic masks and analyze how these masks affect the downstream tracking and reconstruction of dynamic objects. Our approach improves the mean dynamic mask IoU by 35\% over a state-of-the-art baseline on DAVIS, and reduces the average ATE by 59\% on Bonn and 19\% on TUM relative to the SLAM backbone used in our system. The source code will be made publicly available.

### Title:
          F-LIVO: A Feature-Level Geometric Measurement Framework for LiDAR-Inertial-Visual Odometry
 - **Authors:** Xinye Dai, Yapeng Wang, Shujie Zhou, Zihao Wang, Shiqi Zheng, Jiayu Hu, Yusheng Wang, Weiwei Song, Weiming Tang
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Robotics (cs.RO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 Measurement-level LiDAR-Inertial-Visual Odometry (LIVO) can achieve high accuracy, and its practical resource requirements depend on the adopted association and map-maintenance strategy. Persistently attaching dense photometric patches or appearance attributes to mapped LiDAR points can introduce additional storage, association, and update costs. This paper presents F-LIVO, a filter-based LIVO framework that couples a visual-inertial subsystem and a LiDAR-inertial subsystem through feature-level point-to-plane constraints between visual points and LiDAR planes. To preserve cross-modal coupling when visual triangulation is weak, we further recover visual feature depth from the LiDAR map through local plane fitting and ray-plane intersection. In the LiDAR update, the covariance of each point-to-plane residual is adaptively scaled according to local planar reliability, thereby reducing overconfident updates on poorly conditioned surfaces. Because the proposed method relies on sparse geometric features rather than persistent dense photometric map attributes, it retains a compact map representation. Experiments on multiple public datasets show competitive, sequence-dependent accuracy. Across the evaluated degradation cases, F-LIVO maintains continuous estimation with comparatively low accumulated end-to-end drift. Under the evaluated resource configuration, it achieves the lowest mean and peak central processing unit (CPU) usage, the lowest peak resident set size (RSS), and the lowest RSS growth rate among the evaluated implementations, while R2LIVE retains a lower mean RSS. The source code will be made publicly available upon acceptance to facilitate reproducible research.

 - **Abstract**
 Reliable 3D person detection is important for rescue robotics to give first responders situational awareness. Yet most multimodal 3D detection research targets automotive scenarios in which pedestrians mostly appear in a narrow range of upright, near-road poses. It is therefore unclear how much of the reported performance transfers to rescue settings, as a person may be lying, partially occluded, or in unstructured indoor and outdoor environments. To investigate this gap, we introduce TriSAR-3D, a multimodal person detection dataset recorded with a high-resolution 128-channel LiDAR, an RGB camera, and a thermal camera. Additionally, we test how the performance of different 3D object detectors generalizes to unseen pose categories.  - **Authors:** Jae Joong Lee, Bedrich Benes
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 FOR-species20K is the largest public LiDAR benchmark for tree-species classification, pooling 25 independently collected terrestrial, mobile, and airborne laser-scanning datasets across 33 species. We introduce a multi-resolution, multi-view ConvNeXtV2 model that improves official hidden-test accuracy from 79.5% to 85.0%, ranking first at the time of submission. We then examine what this score measures. Because the hidden test is species-balanced, its overall accuracy is more comparable to a macro metric than to accuracy under the public set's long-tailed distribution. Moreover, the official random split places every source dataset in both training and evaluation. Count-matched retraining and experiments with two alternative backbones indicate that this gap is not explained by reduced training support alone, while an external PureForest experiment shows a smaller but consistent grouped-split effect. These results establish the 85% leaderboard score as strong in-distribution performance, but not as an estimate of source-disjoint generalization, motivating balanced metrics and source-aware evaluation. ### Title:
          Frame-Consistent Feed-Forward 3D Gaussian Splatting from Sparse Unposed Images
 - **Authors:** Viruthshaan Vinasirajan, Ranga Rodrigo
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (cs.CV)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 Feed-forward Gaussian models have made novel view synthesis almost instantaneous. Feed-forward methods predict Gaussians directly, either in two stages or in a single merged pass. Using two separately trained models is attractive because each can specialize in its own objective: one for accurate pose estimation and the other for high-quality reconstruction, rather than forcing a single jointly trained network to compromise between the two. A natural instantiation pairs a frozen pose estimator, VGGT, with a frozen reconstruction backbone, TokenGS. Prior work, such as VGGT-X and 3R-GS, also adopts a two-stage design, but attributes the resulting quality degradation to noisy pose estimates and introduces learned refinement. We show that this is not the primary source of the problem. Through per-camera analysis across 107 scenes from two datasets, DL3DV and MipNeRF360, using COLMAP poses as the reference, we find that the rotation error after alignment is small (median 1.6 degrees), whereas the translation scale is offset by a large, dataset-consistent factor. Controlled ablations over 140 DL3DV scenes show that the primary obstacles are two integration failures rather than pose error: a coordinate-frame inconsistency and a translation-scale mismatch, both of which can be corrected deterministically. With these fixes, a frozen VGGT driving a frozen TokenGS achieves 22.47 PSNR, within 0.51 dB of the COLMAP reference, with no additional training, when poses are estimated from all scene views. In the sparse setting, where reconstruction uses only four context images under the standard pose-free protocol, the same pipeline matches this all-view quality, yielding a fully COLMAP-free pipeline without any refinement step.

### Title:

 - **Authors:** Tianyu Wu (Project Aether)
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI); Machine Learning (cs.LG)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 Automated discovery systems propose scientific explanations and test which to accept. Does passing a test establish the intended claim? We develop four executable operations for changing physical descriptions: identifying redundancies (L1), coarse-graining dynamics (L2), transferring structure (L3), and inferring missing quantities from conservation imbalances (L4). ### Title:
          Modeling and Analysis of Dynamic Coupling Effects in a Free-Floating Space Manipulator
 - **Authors:** Erik Chumacero-Polanco
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Systems and Control (eess.SY)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 Motion of a robotic manipulator mounted on a free-floating spacecraft induces base translation and rotation, creating a dynamic coupling that affects end-effector motion. This paper characterizes that coupling in a planar three-link space manipulator. Using momentum conservation, velocity mappings are developed between manipulator joints, the end effector, and the spacecraft base. Singular value decomposition is then used to examine coupling strength, directional anisotropy, and the orientation of the coupled motions. Numerical maps show that coupling strength is invariant to the first joint angle but depends strongly on the relative configuration of the remaining joints. Increasing the spacecraft base mass reduces joint-to-base and end-effector-to-base coupling, while its effect on the joint-to-end-effector mapping is smaller and more dependent on configuration. The translational end-effector-to-base mapping is highly anisotropic in some configurations: its base-velocity ellipse becomes increasingly flattened as the weak coupling mode approaches zero. The principal end-effector and base velocity directions also change their relative alignment across the configuration space, producing predominantly opposing responses in some regions and transverse or approximately codirectional responses in others. Together, these results provide a geometric description of how coupling magnitude and direction vary with manipulator configuration and spacecraft mass.
