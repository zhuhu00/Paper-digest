### Title:
          Zero-Shot Spoken Language Understanding with SLAM-ASR: What Do Monolingual ASR-Trained Linear Projectors Enable?
 - **Authors:** Edwin Arkel Rios, Sneh Sourabh, Jocelyn Zaruma, Girish Ewoorkar, Julian Mack, Bo-Cheng Lai
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Sound (cs.SD); Audio and Speech Processing (eess.AS)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 The SLAM-ASR framework connects the features of a frozen speech encoder as input to a frozen LLM decoder to enable speech transcription. In this work we ask whether this training-on speech transcription-provides the LLM with the capability to also perform spoken language understanding (SLU) tasks, by training monolingual projectors on 11 languages. When prompted to answer a yes/no question about the input speech, the mean instruction following rate (IFR) is 49.8%. When not following the instruction, these models default back to transcription. The transcription quality-measured by the character error rate (CER)-is not sufficient to predict IFR. Remarkably, when the model is instructed to answer the topic question as a single digit option, the mean IFR rises to 81.3%. Our models score a mean 23.2% topic balanced accuracy (BAcc) against 14.3% chance. However, the same decoder scores a mean 71.5% BAcc when using reference transcripts as text input. This indicates a failure of the projector to transfer information for the SLU tasks.
### Title:
          Line-Augmented cuVSLAM: Robust Visual Odometry in Low-Texture Environments
 - **Authors:** Dara Molloy, Terry Wu, Wonju Lee, Abhilash Nand Kumar, David Adley, Conor Ferguson, Hesam Rabeti
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Robotics (cs.RO); Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (cs.CV)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 Autonomous mobile robots in warehouses and production facilities operate in precisely the environments that defeat point-feature visual odometry: featureless aisles, uniform walls, repetitive racking and specular floors. Such environments are, however, rich in straight-edge structure: rack uprights, door frames, wall junctions and cabinet outlines present high-contrast linear edges stable across wide viewpoint changes. A line-feature extension to cuVSLAM, a CUDA-accelerated visual-SLAM system, is presented that recovers this signal. Lines are parameterised as Plücker coordinates for geometric transport and as the minimal 4-DOF orthonormal representation of Bartoli and Sturm for optimisation; a Cauchy-robust point-to-line residual with analytic Jacobians is folded directly into cuVSLAM's per-frame Gauss-Newton pose solve, and the same line state is jointly refined in the Schur-complement sliding-window bundle adjustment. An adaptive information weight causes lines to assume more of the estimation burden exactly as point features become scarce, and the extension adds residual blocks to existing solvers rather than introducing a second estimator, reducing exactly to the unmodified point pipeline when disabled. Evaluated in RGB-D mode under SE(3) Umeyama alignment with no scale correction, line features reduce mean absolute trajectory error by 38.5% over the 22 sequences of the TUM RGB-D Freiburg3 collection and by 30.1% over the 8 Replica room and office sequences, where median translation error falls by 38.9% and median rotation error by 66.4%, with zero tracking failures in any configuration tested.
### Title:
          LIN-SLAM: Lightweight LiDAR--Inertial Neural SLAM for Real-Time Onboard Navigation
 - **Authors:** Shiqi Che, Yijie Liu, Shuo Wang, Wenxin Liu
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Robotics (cs.RO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 Geometric light detection and ranging (LiDAR)--inertial simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) provides efficient state estimation but lacks the continuous map representation and global refinement capabilities of neural implicit SLAM, while neural implicit methods remain computationally expensive. We propose Lightweight LiDAR--Inertial Neural SLAM (LIN-SLAM), a hybrid method that couples high-rate geometric LiDAR--inertial estimation with neural implicit tracking, continual mapping, and global refinement for accurate real-time onboard SLAM. Experiments on twelve representative sequences from three datasets spanning 32-, 64-, and 128-channel LiDAR configurations show that LIN-SLAM achieves the lowest overall mean absolute trajectory error (ATE) among the evaluated state-of-the-art geometric and neural implicit methods, while substantially reducing runtime compared with existing neural implicit SLAM methods. On a Unitree Go2 equipped with an NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX and a 16-channel LiDAR, LIN-SLAM sustains real-time localization and neural mapping over a 1.47-km indoor trajectory through challenging repetitive corridor environments, with an endpoint closure error of only 0.141~m. To the best of our knowledge, LIN-SLAM is the first LiDAR--inertial SLAM system with a neural implicit backend to achieve real-time online operation on a mobile robotic platform while maintaining state-of-the-art localization accuracy. The code will be released upon acceptance.
### Title:
          CLAD: Keypoint Matching Using Global Line Correspondences
 - **Authors:** Masaki Satoh
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (cs.CV); Robotics (cs.RO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 Traditional keypoint descriptors, such as SIFT (Scale-Invariant Feature Transform) and ORB (Oriented FAST and Rotated BRIEF), are computed from local image patches. They may be unreliable in scenes with repetitive patterns or weak textures, where patches at different locations can look similar. We propose the Contextual Line-Assisted Descriptor (CLAD), a keypoint matching method that addresses this problem by using global geometric context from line correspondences. CLAD represents each keypoint with a vector whose elements indicate its proximity to matched lines. It can therefore use the keypoint's spatial relationships to the lines in the scene. We use HOME (Hough-space One-dimensional Matching of Extrema) to detect both clear and weak linear structures and to establish enough line-to-keypoint associations to represent this context. Evaluations on the TUM RGB-D SLAM dataset and outdoor video sequences show that CLAD provides geometrically consistent matches that complement those obtained with traditional local descriptors. In repetitive scenes, where local descriptors may not distinguish similar patches, CLAD uses global structure to improve matching reliability. The method is computationally lightweight and practical to implement.
### Title:
          AVALON: Zero-Shot Indoor Navigation with Human-Seeded Topological Memory
 - **Authors:** Jing Xia, Changyan Li, Yuduo Wang, Guiyin Tian, Bo Wang, Yifan Liao, Mingjun Li, Shiyan Hu
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Robotics (cs.RO); Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 Indoor service robots often enter unmapped buildings whose layouts and task constraints are already familiar to users. We address this human-familiar, robot-unseen setting with AVALON, a topological navigation framework organized around human-seeded layered spatial memory, node-verified topological execution, and task-triggered, evidence-gated human--robot memory co-updating. The robot has no prior on-site traversal or site-specific training. Deployment requires only a floor plan, place clues, and an initial topology-odometry alignment. Visual depth point-goal navigation leverages verified node arrivals to perform translational re-anchoring, without requiring a robot-constructed global SLAM map. Human--robot memory co-updating fuses complementary sources of information. Human operators specify task constraints that are unobservable via vision and report offline environmental modifications, whereas the robot provides physical-state evidence through active in-situ validation. Task relevance governs whether human-reported environmental changes undergo robot validation during the active mission. Field-collected perceptual evidence gates memory updates, maintaining an explicit separation between human-specified permissions and robot-observed traversability. Our experiments on a Unitree Go2 show that AVALON with X-NavDP completes a 12.81 m Intersection-to-Dining mission in 46.4 s (5/5) with no mapping, 40.9% less robot time than DimOS-Go2 (78.5 s, 4/5), while policy-only X-NavDP times out in all of the 5 runs. These demonstrate the superiority of the proposed topological navigation framework.

### Title:
          Can Autonomous Vehicles Keep Their Cool? A Survey of Thermal Side-Channel and Perception Attacks
 - **Authors:** Harsh Aaryan, Ce Zhou
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cryptography and Security (cs.CR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 Autonomous vehicles (AVs) couple safety-critical perception to thermally constrained compute. Interfaces built for night perception and package thermal reliability were never engineered as confidentiality boundaries, and closing one such path leaves the other intact. Physical adversaries can spoof thermal infrared (IR) sensors used for night driving, while unprivileged software co-tenants can fingerprint perception workloads from thermal, dynamic voltage and frequency scaling (DVFS), and thermal event interrupt (TEI) telemetry required for package safety. These paths differ in mechanism and privilege; both exploit interfaces that AV stacks cannot disable without compromising Safety of the Intended Functionality (SOTIF). Classical microarchitectural and cyber-physical systems (CPS) surveys address cache timing, adversarial machine learning, and camera or LiDAR spoofing; no prior survey jointly frames physical thermal IR attacks and unprivileged silicon thermal telemetry as one residual-risk class. This survey systematizes thirteen representative works into thermal perception integrity, telemetry leakage, and model-information extraction, comparing them by privilege, timescale, co-tenancy, deployability, and AV relevance. We distinguish attack family from adversary class and map defenses to residual risk while thermal management remains operational. A commodity proxy supports the software co-tenant branch only; physical IR evidence comes from cited literature. We identify open problems in on-target automotive evaluation, fusion trust under IR spoofing, chiplet thermal coupling, capability-based telemetry, and standards-ready threat analysis, aiming to motivate co-design across sensors, operating systems, silicon, and automotive standards.

### Title:
          Scene Reconstruction Using Ubiquitous Wireless Signals via Diffusion Posterior Inference Over physics-Grounded Representations
 - **Authors:** Vahid Yazdnian, Yasaman Ghasempour
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Machine Learning (cs.LG)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 Knowing the layout of the physical world matters for many applications, yet the sensors we rely on for it (e.g., cameras and LiDAR) can fail in darkness, in adverse weather, or behind walls. Wireless signals do not face these limitations: every link between a base station and a user is shaped by the buildings it passes, reflects from, or is blocked by, leaving information about the surrounding scene embedded in the wireless channel measured at the endpoints. However, mapping channel measurements to the environment is a severely ill-posed problem: a measurement records only the structures that interact with the transmitted electromagnetic waves along its propagation paths, and even these observations can often be explained by multiple plausible scenes. We therefore formulate wireless scene reconstruction as posterior inference and develop a hierarchical framework with two complementary stages to approach this problem. A wireless foundation model, pretrained to respect propagation physics, transforms each channel measurement into a representation of the scene structure that shaped the observation. A diffusion model then jointly integrates representations from multiple measurements to model and sample the posterior over building occupancy, with consensus over samples producing the final reconstruction. We demonstrate that the framework can accurately recover building footprints from wireless channel measurements alone and show that its performance depends critically on both design components: physics-grounded representations expose scene-relevant information, while joint posterior inference coherently integrates complementary observations.
### Title:
          FutureLift: Lifting Trajectory Proposals into Bidirectional Future Graphs for Motion Forecasting
 - **Authors:** Daniel Grimm, J. Marius Zöllner
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (cs.CV); Machine Learning (cs.LG)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 Accurate motion prediction is critical for safe autonomous driving, yet even strong forecasters leave residual errors in their multimodal proposals. Existing refiners compress complete proposals or update their segments sequentially, preventing the full proposed future from serving as explicit relational context for correction. We introduce FutureLift, a backbone-agnostic refiner that represents each multimodal proposal as a heterogeneous future graph, with spatially grounded temporal nodes connected to agent histories, map elements, alternative modes, and, for joint prediction, other agents future trajectories. Bidirectional temporal edges allow later evidence to revise earlier segments in a single pass, and the updated nodes predict residual coordinate and confidence corrections. Requiring only proposals, scores, and scene context, FutureLift provides a common refinement interface for structurally different forecasting backbones. On Argoverse2 and the Waymo Open Motion Dataset, it consistently improves frozen proposals and outperforms native and specialized refiners across marginal and joint forecasting. On the WOMD motion-prediction leaderboard, FutureLift ranks first in minADE (0.5049m) and minFDE (0.9942m) among the compared standard-input methods, without LiDAR or ensembling, while achieving a competitive 0.4874 Soft mAP.

 - **Authors:** Shahryar G. Namdari, Francesco Iacomi, Riccardo Barbieri, Luca Mainardi

 - **Abstract**
 Imagined speech decoding from EEG offers a promising route toward naturalistic brain-computer interfaces. Traditional closed-set classifiers treat semantic categories as independent labels and do not explicitly exploit relationships among linguistic intents, limiting output flexibility. We adapt a multimodal contrastive learning framework to imagined speech, aligning EEG trials with language representations in the embedding space of a frozen pretrained text encoder. An EEG encoder is trained with a composite objective combining semantically reweighted EEG-sentence alignment and text-prototype-based class discrimination, encouraging semantic structure while preserving separability among 39 intent categories. ### Title:
          Cyclotron: A Recurrence Language for Expressing Interprocessor Communication
 - **Authors:** Shiv Sundram, Akhilesh Balasingam, Nathan Zhang, Kunle Olukotun, Fredrik Kjolstad
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Distributed, Parallel, and Cluster Computing (cs.DC)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 We present Cyclotron, a compiler that expresses streaming dataflow algorithms using recurrence equations, which are then compiled to distributed topologies of interlinked processors. Our compiler lowers a language of recurrences over logical iteration spaces into per-processor programs of sends, receives, and computations. In these recurrences, data accesses are not limited to external accesses to memory; data can live separately on the iteration space itself, allowing internal points to communicate values directly with other points. Users specify this communication directly in the recurrence language, which allows them to express various communication patterns such as a nearest-neighbor systolic exchange, a one-to-all broadcast, a bidirectional split ring, or an asymmetric and piecewise broadcast pattern, which are often not supported by standard distributed linear algebra libraries. These recurrences describe the algorithm and communication independently of how they map onto the hardware. A programmer then chooses which dimensions execute across processors and which execute over time, without rewriting the entire program. Users only specify data dependencies across the iteration space, and the compiler safely generates a program, per process, with correct placement of both communication and computational operators according to the chosen space--time mapping. We evaluate distributed matrix multiplication, TRSM, Cholesky, and fused Cholesky--TRSM on up to 16 CPU nodes and 16 GPUs, comparing against ScaLAPACK, SLATE, and cuSolverMp. Cyclotron is competitive with and often outperforms these hand-optimized libraries; for Cholesky, the best communication strategy changes with problem size and architecture.
