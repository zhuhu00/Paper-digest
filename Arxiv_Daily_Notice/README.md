### Title:
          Bracketing Shows the Way: Adaptive Multi-Exposure Policy as Input for Robust Visual SLAM
 - **Authors:** Olivier Gamache, Anthony Lavertu, David-Alexandre Duclos, Jean-Michel Fortin, François Pomerleau, Philippe Giguère
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Robotics (cs.RO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 Rapid illumination transitions can disturb Visual SLAM (VSLAM) by saturating images before the Automatic Exposure (AE) policy adapts, removing the visual correspondences needed to maintain tracking. Our proposed methods mitigate this disturbance at two levels: B-Adapt acquires adaptive exposure brackets to cover the scene dynamic range while promoting photometric overlap, and BVSLAM directly uses the multi-exposure sequence as constraints in a factor graph state estimator without relying on High Dynamic Range (HDR) merging. We evaluate both methods against state-of-the-art approaches in nominal environments and during a repeated tunnel-exit transition at an emulated vehicle speed of 32 m s-1. In the tunnel, combining our B-Adapt with BVSLAM reduces the state estimation error by 78 % compared with AE images. These results suggest that bracketing efficiently improves visual pose estimation robustness to rapid illumination disturbances while retaining low error under nominal conditions. ### Title:
          T-ConSG: A LiDAR-Based Real-Time Temporal Construction Scene Graph for Dynamic and Evolving Environments
 - **Authors:** Yilong Chen, Yong K. Cho
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Robotics (cs.RO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 Dynamic and continuously evolving environments present significant challenges for robotic mapping and scene understanding, particularly on construction sites where moving agents, temporary obstacles, and persistent structural changes coexist. While dynamic and long-term SLAM methods can detect motion and environmental changes, their outputs are predominantly metric representations that provide limited structure for higher-level spatial and temporal reasoning. This paper presents a LiDAR-based real-time temporal construction scene graph (T-ConSG) that jointly represents free-space regions, static objects, dynamic objects, and environmental events with spatial-relational and event-participation edges. The proposed framework incrementally constructs the graph from LiDAR-derived geometric evidence, maintaining persistent free-space and static-object information while tracking dynamic objects. The results demonstrate that the proposed T-ConSG provides a unified representation for organizing spatial and temporal information and supports robotic reasoning across environments containing both short-term dynamics and persistent changes.
### Title:
          PosePlaner: Denoising Any Feed-Forward Pose Predictor from Pairwise Planar Geometry
 - **Authors:** Yihang Chen, Bin Tan, Yujun Shen, Yinghao Xu, Nan Xue
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (cs.CV)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 Recent multi-view Transformers (e.g., DUSt3R and VGGT) have advanced 3D reconstruction by scaling training data and model capacity from two views to many views and long sequences, subsuming the classical SfM/SLAM pipeline of feature extraction, matching, and pose estimation in a single feed-forward pass. Yet this subsumption hides the interfaces between pipeline stages---correspondences, geometry, and pose are entangled in a single forward pass, leaving supervised fine-tuning as the major path to improvement and offering no mechanism to correct predictions from geometric evidence at test time. We propose PosePlaner to close this gap by reviving the classical coupling between correspondences and pose via flow matching, treating pose refinement as a conditional generation problem that takes coarse geometric matches and initial pose as input condition and fuses the resulting pose hypotheses into a single refined estimate, providing a continuous, data-driven analog of RANSAC voting. Our method is trained efficiently on two-view data, yet serves as a periodic refiner for streaming n-view pose estimators with no retraining. Empirically, it corrects failures of feed-forward predictors, substantially improves correspondence-based solvers, and reduces accumulated drift in long streaming sequences.

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/

 - **Abstract**
 Visual Place Recognition (VPR) is an important means for loop closure detection in visual Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM). While state-of-the-art VPR and SLAM approaches excel in urban and feature-rich natural environments, the vast and feature-poor environments of extraterrestrial surfaces like on the Moon or Mars pose severe challenges for existing approaches. Under this GT we require a VPR method to identify exactly those image pairs for which a subsequently applied RPE is expected to provide a valuable pose estimate in a SLAM context. (3) We evaluated nine state-of-the-art VPR approaches. The results show considerable room for improvement and indicate directions for future research.  - **Authors:** Xiao'ao Song, Chahat Deep Singh

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 High-performance optical flow networks are difficult to deploy on resource-constrained edge accelerators because their iterative refinement warps features by the current flow, which relies on flow-dependent resampling and data-dependent memory access. We present TinyFlow, a 1.26M-parameter warp-free optical flow network that preserves iterative coarse-to-fine refinement by replacing the warp with a fixed-window correlation over constant offsets, so the whole network compiles as a single static INT8 graph.  - **Abstract**
 Event cameras provide microsecond temporal resolution, high dynamic range, and sparse asynchronous measurements, making them compelling for odometry under fast motion and challenging illumination. Yet, accurate event-based state estimation remains difficult: existing systems often use events as a complement to frames or rely on feature tracking, correspondence search, and optimization-heavy backends, limiting the efficiency promised by event sensing. ### Title:

 - **Authors:** Kulbhushan Komarraju, Nathaniel Pearson, Yu Gu, Jason Gross
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 Floor traction influences wheel-slip behavior, dead-reckoning accuracy, and the navigation performance of wheeled mobile robots that rely on wheel odometry. As these robots become increasingly common in GNSS-denied indoor environments, including automated manufacturing and retail facilities, their onboard measurements could also be used to characterize floor conditions and improve localization. In this study, a skid-steer robot was driven over five floor surfaces with distinct traction characteristics using commanded velocity profiles designed to induce wheel slip through repeated acceleration and direction reversal. Differences between the body velocity estimated from inertial sensing and the velocity obtained from wheel odometry were evaluated using the integrated relative-velocity error, phase-derived wheel-to-body response delay, and velocity-reversal response delay. The response-delay measurements produced consistent surface-dependent trends, with the longest delays observed on soap and the shortest delays observed on carpet. These results demonstrate that both the magnitude and frequency domain characteristics of wheel-to-body velocity mismatch contain useful information about tire-surface interactions. Such information could support surface recognition and the development of slip-aware wheel-odometry weighting within robot state-estimation frameworks.

As a result, OSWAM can efficiently and effectively generate high quality actions in just one diffusion step. Our experiments demonstrate that OSWAM achieves comparable or even better results in both autonomous driving and embodied control than direct-policy WAM and Joint-WAM that require tens of steps. It obtains approximately $3\times$ speedup over direct-policy WAM baselines and supports real-time compiled inference with latency as low as 40ms on an NVIDIA RTX 5090 GPU.
