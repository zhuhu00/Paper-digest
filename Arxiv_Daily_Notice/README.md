### Title:
          Reading the Room: Scale-ambiguous Semantic Registration of Sparse VSLAM Maps to Floor Plans in Partially Built Environments
 - **Authors:** Bjorn Stragier, Emilia Szymańska, J. Micah Prendergast, Lukas Rosenberger Schmid
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Robotics (cs.RO); Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (cs.CV)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 Many robotic construction applications, such as progress tracking, require registering a sensor trajectory to a prior 2D floor plan. Visual capture is attractive for its low cost and availability, but aligning a visual simultaneous localization and mapping (VSLAM) map to a floor plan is difficult; VSLAM recovers geometry only up to an unknown similarity transform, the sparse point cloud is cross-modal to the symbolic floor plan, sites are only partially built during capture, and construction sites are visually repetitive, degrading map quality while removing distinctive features. Existing methods address at most two of these challenges. We thus introduce Reading the Room(RR), a global-local approach to register scale-ambiguous, sparse, semantic VSLAM maps to partially built floor plans. Our key idea is to define a single robust, differentiable, class-balanced semantic-geometric energy function that governs all stages of the pipeline. We develop two global initialization schemes, leveraging a single inaccurate user-provided correspondence if provided, or estimating the initialization globally using a cross-modal matching network. Both schemes are designed to find initial candidates with respect to the energy function, which is then directly optimized for local refinement. We evaluate RR on 33 sequences across three active construction sites against classical and learning-based baselines, achieving a 4.5x improvement in mean absolute trajectory error over the strongest baseline in the general unconstrained setting, which increases to 7.6x when an anchor is provided.
### Title:
          DewHand: Direct Metric World-Space Hand Motion Reconstruction from Egocentric Videos
 - **Authors:** Naru Suzuki, Satoshi Ikehata, Takehiko Ohkawa, Yoichi Sato, Shuhei Kurita
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (cs.CV)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 Recovering metric world-space hand motion from egocentric video supports understanding human interactions and transferring them to embodied agents. Three challenges complicate this task: multi-stage pipelines propagate errors from independently estimated hand and camera motion; monocular observations leave physical scale ambiguous; and occlusion and out-of-view hands admit multiple plausible motion trajectories. We present DewHand, a unified model for direct joint reconstruction of world-space hand and camera motion. To avoid compounding errors from multi-stage estimation, DewHand jointly predicts hand and camera motion in a shared world coordinate system within a single-stage pipeline. To resolve metric-scale ambiguity, it combines scene-level geometric features from VGGT-$\Omega$ with metric-space supervision, while hand-specific features preserve fine-grained articulation. To handle occlusion and out-of-view hands, we formulate reconstruction as conditional motion generation with flow matching. Under the same architecture, this generative formulation outperforms direct regression in both accuracy and temporal smoothness with only two sampling steps. Experiments on HOT3D and ARCTIC demonstrate improved world-space accuracy and temporal consistency over state-of-the-art methods. DewHand reduces rigid-aligned world-space error relative to StableHand from 32.9 to 28.4 mm on HOT3D and from 76.2 to 36.2 mm on ARCTIC. It also recovers camera scale within 10% on two thirds of HOT3D clips, versus about a quarter for SLAM. Moreover, in zero-shot evaluations on HoloAssist, H2O, and TACO, captured with different devices, DewHand achieves the lowest rigid-aligned world-space error (WA-MPJPE) on each dataset.

### Title:
          A2I-Calib++: A Kinematics-Driven Anti-noise Active Calibration Framework for Whole-Body Multi-IMU Systems on Legged Robots
 - **Authors:** Fangyu Jiang, Chaoran Xiong, Kehui Ma, Zhen Sun, Zeyu Zhang, Ling Pei
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Robotics (cs.RO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 Recently, multi-node inertial measurement unit (multi-IMU) systems have shown promise for robust odometry in legged robots. Multi-IMU fusion can improve legged robot odometry but requires accurate spatiotemporal calibration among the sensors. Existing multi-IMU calibration methods, designed for aerial or handheld platforms, face challenges on legged robots because routine locomotion may provide insufficient excitation, while foot slips, impacts, and vibrations introduce measurement disturbances. To address these challenges, we propose A2I-Calib++, a kinematics-driven anti-noise active calibration framework for body-mounted IMUs covered by the robot kinematic model. It estimates temporal offsets, extrinsic rotations and translations, and inertial biases through temporal correlation search followed by analytic spatial and bias estimation, without artificial calibration targets or complex parameter tuning for the target IMU. A2I-Calib++ comprises three core modules: 1) a Noise-Isotropy-Inspired Trajectory supported by a finite-horizon conditioning proposition that generates sufficient excitation to limit noise sensitivity; 2) a constraint-aware Adversarial Motion Priors controller that combines the anti-noise excitation with a rolling-contact reward for contact-aware execution; and 3) a contact-constrained virtual IMU that fuses pre-calibrated calf IMUs and joint kinematics under a rolling-contact model to construct the calibration reference. Furthermore, evaluations on both simulation and real-world quadruped platforms demonstrate notably reduced noise sensitivity and simulation calibration errors compared to conventional methods, together with repeatable real-world calibration and improved proprioceptive odometry accuracy for legged robots.

This paper presents VeriCalib, which formulates extrinsic calibration as a verifiable estimation problem and reports a calibration state.  - **Abstract**
 Autonomous mobile robots operating in dense crowds and cluttered sites require $360^\circ$ perception to react to surrounding hazards. While panoramic LiDAR offers full-surround coverage, learning representations within reinforcement learning (RL) is sample-inefficient under sparse rewards, and existing 3D pretrained point-cloud encoders are too memory-heavy for massively parallel RL simulation. We present RangeNav, a compact (183k parameters) LiDAR range-image encoder pretrained entirely on synthetic data. ### Title:
          DriftNeRF: Cross-Ray Density Drifting for Sparse-View Neural Radiance Fields
 - **Authors:** Juntang Wang, Yihan Wang, Sheng'en Li, Hao Wu, Dongmian Zou, Shixin Xu
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (cs.CV); Machine Learning (cs.LG)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) synthesize photorealistic novel views from dense image collections, but degrade sharply when few views are available. We attribute a specific, measurable defect to the objective itself: the rendering loss is a sum over independent rays and contains no term comparing densities that different rays assign to the same region of space, so nearby 3D points can settle on contradictory densities while every training pixel is reproduced correctly. We make this quantity explicit by measuring cross-ray density disagreement directly during training, and show that it stays high in vanilla NeRF, falls only incidentally under frequency-based regularization, and moves together with held-out quality in the sparse regime. We then close the gap with DriftNeRF, a drop-in regularizer that instantiates the drifting-field template on density samples: a masked exponential kernel aggregates densities from nearby points on other rays into a stop-gradient consensus target, and the resulting drifting field penalizes unsupported disagreement while leaving agreed-upon boundaries intact. The regularizer reads only sampled positions, ray identifiers, and predicted densities, so it requires no pretrained model, external geometry, or additional images, and transfers across field parameterizations. On NeRF-Synthetic it reduces residual disagreement by an order of magnitude, and across NeRF-Synthetic, DTU, and LLFF it improves 3-view PSNR over the strongest MLP-NeRF baseline by 0.42-0.65 dB and remains competitive with sparse-view Gaussian pipelines under a matched protocol without their depth, flow, or covisibility preprocessing.
### Title:
          Progressive Permutation Alignment with Temperature-Controlled Spectral Sharpening
 - **Authors:** Yuta Horioka, Naoya Chiba, Yuki Uranishi
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Machine Learning (cs.LG); Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (cs.CV)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 In implicit neural representations (INRs), 3D signals are encoded directly in network weights, making stable weight-space operations essential. However, permutation symmetry causes independently trained yet functionally equivalent models to appear distant in weight space, often inducing large loss barriers under linear interpolation. Existing neuron alignment methods either commit early to discrete matches and become trapped in inconsistent local optima or rely on computationally expensive gradient optimization over continuous relaxations, both of which are unreliable in challenging cases such as NeRF. We adopt progressive alignment, a layer-wise strategy that follows inter-layer dependencies from input to output. At each layer, a correlation matrix is estimated and aligned with a discrete anchor obtained by the Hungarian algorithm, after which temperature-controlled spectral annealing gradually sharpens the correlation structure toward a permutation matrix. This update is deterministic and lightweight, avoiding the inner-loop gradient optimization required by continuous relaxation methods. A final refinement stage further optimizes the network layer-by-layer via weight matching, starting from the aligned solution. Experiments on MLPs, CNNs, and Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) show that the proposed method reliably recovers neuron correspondences and achieves stable alignment across architectures. In practical experiments aligning independently trained NeRF models, our method reduces the loss barriers under weight interpolation compared with existing approaches.

 - **Authors:** Diego Sanches Nere dos Santos, Luiz Carlos Bambirra Torres

 - **Abstract**
 The Gabriel graph is a proximity graph widely used in computational geometry and geometric machine learning, underlying large-margin classifiers and, as we show in this work, density-aware clustering methods. Its construction, however, has cubic time complexity $O(N^3)$ in the naive formulation, which severely limits its use on datasets beyond a few thousand samples. This paper presents a set of GPU parallelization strategies, implemented in CUDA, that make Gabriel graph construction practical at scale.
