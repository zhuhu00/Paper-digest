### Title:
          The Rise of Personal Trackers as the Next Infrastructure for Ubiquitous Positioning
 - **Authors:** Mohamed I. Hany, Hamada Rizk, Moustafa Youssef
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Networking and Internet Architecture (cs.NI)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 Reliable indoor positioning remains a central unsolved problem in spatial computing. Existing systems depend on fixed, provider-owned infrastructure (WiFi access points, Bluetooth beacons, UWB anchors) whose deployment, calibration, and ownership models limit their scalability. Meanwhile, personal trackers, wearables, smart luggage tags, and tagged industrial assets are becoming densely embedded in the very indoor environments where positioning infrastructure is weakest. This paper argues that these devices point to a new class of spatial infrastructure: non-stationary, user-owned, and semantically interpretable. We present TrackerNet, a vision for transforming personal trackers from private object-finders into a decentralized substrate for ubiquitous positioning. The key shift is to stop treating anchor mobility as noise: TrackerNet models mobile anchors as probabilistic references whose stability, trust, and semantic role are inferred over time, reframing indoor localization as non-stationary spatial graph inference. We identify three research frontiers (non-stationary SLAM over mobile, intermittently visible anchors; semantic reasoning that connects ranging signals to interpretations of anchor behavior; and consent-based, privacy-preserving governance for cross-vendor cooperation) and a staged pathway by which the vendors and venue operators who already run tracker networks can realize them with academia. We do not claim a finished system; we define a research agenda.

### Title:

 - **Abstract**
 Reconstructing human activity from egocentric video requires more than recovering individual 3D components: the observer's motion, surrounding geometry, and hand-object interactions must fit together in a consistent world. Achieving this coherence requires both joint reconstruction and aligned supervision, yet existing methods and datasets offer limited support for either. We present EgoXperience, a full-stack paradigm that addresses these needs through data collection, automatic annotation, unified modeling, and benchmarking. ### Title:
          CAOS-SLAM: Class-Agnostic Object-Aware Real-Time vSLAM for Dynamic Environments
 - **Authors:** Gaia Borghi, João Carlos Virgolino Soares, Angelo Bratta, Shahab Heshmati-Alamdari, Claudio Semini
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Robotics (cs.RO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 Robust visual Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (vSLAM) in dynamic environments faces challenges due to the presence of moving objects that may introduce erroneous observations, corrupt the map reconstruction and overall degrade localization accuracy. Existing vSLAM approaches often rely on predefined semantic classes or costly pixel-wise classification, limiting their robustness or applicability in real-world scenarios. This paper presents CAOS-SLAM, a dynamic vSLAM framework that extends ORB-SLAM3 with a hybrid perception pipeline to detect, track, and filter both semantically known and previously unseen moving objects in real-time. On-demand panoptic segmentation is performed for object discovery, with candidate unknown objects instantiated through depth-based clustering of unclassified regions. Between panoptic updates, persistent object tracks are updated using lightweight detection when available or temporal mask propagation based on world-frame Kalman prediction, depth consistency, and appearance similarity. The estimated object motion is then used to reject dynamic features from the SLAM pipeline. Experimental results on multiple dynamic RGB-D datasets and real-world experiments demonstrate improved localization accuracy over state-of-the-art dynamic vSLAM approaches, achieving up to 59% lower localization error in scenes characterized by unknown dynamic objects and up to 88% lower error in heavily occluded scenes, while achieving a median processing rate of 30 FPS on a laptop. Furthermore, the tracking module provides estimates of object trajectories and velocities, supporting potential future applications in perception, navigation, and motion-planning tasks in dynamic environments. Code and dataset will be released upon acceptance.

 - **Authors:** Yiming Shu, Peng Sun, Lang Zhang, Mingyue Feng, Yaonong Wang, Pei Liu, Chen Sun, Jun Ma

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 Feedforward 3D reconstruction has largely followed an {appearance-driven paradigm}: multi-view images are encoded into visual representations, cross-view correspondence is implicitly inferred from learned features, and 3D primitives are subsequently predicted from these representations. However, calibrated cameras and sparse metric measurements already provide explicit constraints on where the same physical point should appear across views. We propose a novel paradigm, \textbf{Geometry-Conditioned Feedforward Reconstruction}, in which metric geometry establishes cross-view correspondence before the 3D representation is formed, while learned visual features refine rather than discover this correspondence. Based on this paradigm, we introduce \textbf{GeoForward}. Sparse LiDAR points are unprojected into 3D and analytically projected across calibrated cameras to provide metric correspondence anchors. A bounded continuous local search then refines these anchors using visual features, and the resulting geometry-conditioned representation is fused with global multi-view context from VGGT. The shared representation drives separate metric-depth and Gaussian-attribute prediction, establishing a direct path from explicit geometry to both Gaussian centers and spatial attributes. A sparse 3D refinement module subsequently improves local attribute compatibility while preserving the geometrically determined centers. For temporal reconstruction, endpoint Gaussians are composed using vehicle masks and motion priors. On nuScenes, GeoForward achieves the lowest cross-camera depth-consistency error and Chamfer distance among the compared methods while maintaining competitive reconstruction quality. These results demonstrate that explicitly conditioning correspondence formation on metric geometry provides an effective alternative to purely appearance-driven feedforward 3D reconstruction.
