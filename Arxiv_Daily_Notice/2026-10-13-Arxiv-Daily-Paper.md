### Title:
          MemReg: Streaming Outdoor LiDAR Point Cloud Registration with Hybrid Memory Buffers
 - **Authors:** Jiuming Liu, Mengmeng Liu, Guangming Wang, Lihao Liu, Michael Ying Yang, Yunpeng Zhang, Francesco Nex, Hao Cheng, Hesheng Wang
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (cs.CV)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 Prior outdoor LiDAR registration works are commonly limited by the pair-wise input paradigm, which neglects temporal correlations inherent within streaming sequences. In this paper, we propose a novel multi-frame outdoor point cloud registration network for streaming LiDAR scans enhanced with pose-related memory buffers. The key observation is that long-term temporal LiDAR sequences can provide rich global contextual information to complete sparse measurements, filter outliers, and address low-overlap problems, thereby boosting registration performance. Specifically, two hybrid memory buffers are designed, including an implicit memory feature buffer and an explicit memory pose buffer, to store and dynamically update pose-related temporal features. Moreover, a novel dynamic history weighting module is developed to adaptively fuse current and history pose-related features. Extensive experiments on three outdoor datasets, including KITTI, nuScenes, and Apollo-Southbay, demonstrate state-of-the-art performance of MemReg, surpassing all previous pair-wise methods and also multi-frame SLAM systems. Our method also generalizes surprisingly well to multiview indoor registration scenarios with rather competitive performance on 3DMatch, 3DLoMatch, and ScanNet. ### Title:
          HorizonNav: Geometry-Guided Video Diffusion for Anticipatory 3D Robot Navigation
 - **Authors:** Hajira Amjad, Valerii Serpiva, Viacheslav Liubimov, Didar Seyidov, Dzmitry Tsetserukou
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Robotics (cs.RO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 We present HorizonNav, a video diffusion-based framework for anticipatory robot navigation that transforms a single RGB image and a text prompt into a geometrically aligned representation of plausible surrounding 3D space. Given the initial observation and a textual description of the reconstruction objective, a vision-language model, guided by designed instructions, interprets the scene and constructs structured prompts for a video diffusion model. The resulting video sequence provides plausible future observations of the environment, which are subsequently processed using monocular ORB-SLAM3 for camera pose estimation and Video Depth Anything for dense depth prediction. Our central contribution is a geometry-guided depth alignment stage that combines the estimated camera poses with triangulated visual landmarks to constrain dense depth predictions across multiple views. This alignment improves the geometric consistency of the reconstructed 3D environment, addressing inconsistencies that temporal coherence of the generated video alone cannot resolve. The experimental evaluation of navigation in imagined 3D environment shows that across a 19-scene horizon study, planning-feasible scenes increase from 13/19 at P20 to 15/19 at P81, while successful RRT trials increase from 62/90 to 75/90. In a separate corridor study, 6 of 25 evaluable generated-camera trajectories violate the reconstructed collision constraints; RRT planner recovers feasible alternatives in 5 of those 6 cases. A larger archive contains 58 usable reconstructions from 67 attempted cases, of which 36 support a downstream plan and 22 do not, exposing reconstruction and free-space failure modes rather than hiding them without lidar. Across 15 corridor reconstructions, the mean reconstruction error is approximately 0.30 m at matched structural landmarks.

### Title:
          Reference-Free Food Volume Estimation from Egocentric Stereo Video
 - **Authors:** Vivan Kumar, Gautham Vinod, Fengqing Zhu
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (cs.CV)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 Current stereo vision-based approaches for food portion estimation are hindered by the user burden of using a mobile phone to capture multiple images or placing a reference object in the scene for scale calibration. As a step towards passive dietary intake monitoring, we introduce a method that uses stereo video from a wearable egocentric device to reconstruct food items in true physical scale to accurately estimate volume. By utilizing FoundationStereo for zero-shot stereo matching, SAM 2 for food segmentation, and TSDF fusion over a set of keyframes, our method yields a visually and metrically accurate 3D mesh of any plated food item from a single stereo recording. ### Title:
          When LiDAR Fades, Radar Takes Over: LiDAR Reliability-Aware Adaptive Localization Under Airborne Obscurants
 - **Authors:** Moumita Mukherjee, Avijit Banerjee, Anton Koval, George Nikolakopoulo
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Robotics (cs.RO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 LiDAR-inertial odometry (LIO) is widely used for real-time localization and mapping, but its reliability can degrade substantially in subterranean environments affected by smoke, dust, and other airborne obscurants, whereas radar remains comparatively robust. This article presents a one-class temporal learning framework for LiDAR degradation detection and reliability-aware localization under such conditions. A broad set of degradation-sensitive cues, including spatial, cross-modal, geometric/motion, intensity, and measurement-recency features, is evaluated to characterize changes in LiDAR sensing quality. Their temporal evolution is modelled over sliding windows using an LSTM autoencoder trained exclusively on nominal operation, enabling deviations from healthy sensor behaviour to be detected without requiring labelled degradation data. The resulting LiDAR reliability estimate governs adaptive selection between LIO and radar inertial odometry (RIO), retaining LIO during reliable sensing and transferring localization authority to RIO when degradation is detected. The framework is evaluated using real-world ground robot data collected in GNSS-denied subterranean tunnels under nominal and dense-smoke conditions. Experimental results demonstrate degradation detection, adaptive modality switching, and continued localization output under severe LiDAR degradation. A feature family ablation study further shows that a reduced feature representation can improve degradation-aware localization performance in the evaluated dense-smoke scenarios, highlighting the importance of feature parsimony in one-class sensor degradation detection. The code will be released as open source to support reproducibility and further research.
### Title:
          Mapless, Stabilizing, and Anti-Stuck Flipper Control for Articulated Tracked Robots
 - **Authors:** Valentýn Číhala, Pavel Sarga, Martin Pecka, Tomáš Svoboda, Karel Zimmermann
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Robotics (cs.RO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 Nearly every autonomous or semi-autonomous flipper controller assumes access to an elevation map of the terrain under and in front of the robot. The terrain beneath the chassis is occluded in the sensors, so the map is remembered through odometry integration, whose drift corrupts it. We present Stabilizing Anti-Stuck (SAS), a mapless controller that combines a stabilization term for body roll and pitch with an anti-stuck term that frees the robot when it gets stuck. As input, SAS uses the IMU, the flipper joint angles, and a motion cue that only tells whether the robot is moving. The cue is computed over a short window and never accumulated, so SAS does not inherit the odometry-integration drift that corrupts the map. We deploy SAS on a tracked robot with four flippers and evaluate it against manual teleoperation and prior flipper controllers in a large comparison, both in simulation and on a real obstacle. In simulation and on the real robot, SAS achieves traversal quality comparable to state-of-the-art map-based controllers. On a log hidden in tall grass, invisible to the LiDAR, the best map-based controller gets stuck while SAS traverses the hidden obstacle without a problem.

### Title:
          ROSETTA: Rotation Subspace Estimation for Training-free Transmodal Alignment
 - **Authors:** Alex Junho Lee
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (cs.CV)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 When a query must be matched against a database built with a different sensor or processed by a different encoder, the independently trained encoders impose distinct coordinate systems on the feature space. No canonical reference frame exists to which all encoders can be aligned, and no single training procedure has fully closed this gap. We show that the relative transformation between any two such systems is a spatially preserving rotation in whitened feature space, whose angular velocity exhibits white-noise characteristics: large in norm, temporally uncorrelated, and unpredictable from the features themselves. However, the cross-covariance between encoder outputs has low effective rank, constraining only a small fraction of the rotation parameters, therefore local alignment is tractable. ROSETTA is a training-free calibration layer that estimates the local rotation via an expectation-maximization procedure, requiring no coobservation at query time. We validate ROSETTA on three public driving datasets spanning LiDAR, camera, thermal, and event modalities in cross-modal, cross-device, cross-representation, and cross-backbone settings, with milliseconds overhead per query.

          MeshBind: Point-to-Mesh Binding for Non-Rigid Scene Flow Automatic Annotation

Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (cs.CV)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/
 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/
 - **Abstract**
 Scene flow estimation is essential for many autonomous driving (AD) and robotic applications, but obtaining ground-truth scene flow for non-rigid objects such as pedestrians is highly difficult. We introduce a role-aware transformer architecture that maps the four SEPF dimensions to theory-grounded Semantic Role Labeling (SRL) configurations, incorporating these configurations directly into the transformer's self-attention mechanism. Role-aware attention models non-directional internal states (sentiment and emotion), while directional role-aware attention captures initiator-to-recipient relations for external cues (politeness and formality). Additional evaluation on real-world data demonstrates that the framework improves both predictive performance and model traceability. These results demonstrate that linguistically grounded modifications to transformer self-attention improve the detection of communicative meaning, generalize beyond benchmark datasets to real-world mental-health data, and yield traceable, role-grounded explanations that extend beyond predictive performance to interpretability.
